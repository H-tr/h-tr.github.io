# Solutions in robotics

The third section says how people in robotics address the problem.

## Grouping

- Group the work by mechanism, in the terms of the [formulation](problem.md#formulation). Do
  not group by year, venue or research group.
- Open each group with one sentence on the shared mechanism.
- Close each group with the open part of the group, in concrete terms. Say which part of the
  formulation stays unaddressed.

## Facts for each work

- Choose a small fixed set of facts and give the same facts for every work. The facts follow
  from the formulation. Example for a survey on verification, "the output of the model, the
  check on the output, the trusted remainder".
- Use a table when a group has many works, and prose when the works of a group need
  comparison.
- When a paper does not state one of the facts, derive the fact from the method section and
  mark the fact as a reading of the survey.
- Report limits stated by the authors, quoted where possible.
- Do not rank papers by praise. Say what each paper establishes and what each paper does not.

## Coverage

- Include every paper on the topic ([`CLAUDE.md`](../../CLAUDE.md)). Do not pad with
  off-topic entries.
- Give a classical, pre-foundation-model method as the baseline of a group where one exists.
- Describe a paper in full once. Later sections refer to the paper by name.

## Adding a work

1. Read the abstract page, and the full text for any number
   ([literature](literature.md#before-a-source-enters-the-survey)).
2. Place the work in the group of the matching mechanism.
3. Fill the fixed facts.
4. Add the work to the source index.
5. Check whether the work fills or narrows a [gap](challenges.md#gaps).
